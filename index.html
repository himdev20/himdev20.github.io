Research

Whose words move rates? RBI MPC minutes and Indian yields

Ongoing, since August 2026. With Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur

Do the individual views recorded in the RBI Monetary Policy Committee minutes move Indian interest rates? I parse every MPC statement since 2016, score each member’s hawkishness with a language model, and separate policy shocks from RBI information effects.

Details and findings
  • Scraped all 62 RBI MPC minutes from 2016 to 2026 and parsed them into 372 member statements with votes, in a tested Python pipeline that rebuilds every result from raw RBI, FBIL and FRED data.
  • Scored each statement’s hawkishness with a name-masked LLM that matches the member’s actual vote in all 372 cases and flags the 40 formal dissenters with an AUC of 0.98, benchmarked against dictionary and VADER scores.
  • Separated policy shocks from information effects using Gürkaynak–Sack–Swanson factors and Jarociński–Karadi sign restrictions on OIS and T-bill yields; 42% of decisions carry an information effect.
  • A 1-point hawkish gap between members and the Governor lifts the 1-year OIS rate by 2.3 bp on minutes day (p = 0.015), driven by internal RBI members, with null placebos but a weaker effect outside 2020–21.

Effect of trade policy and geopolitical risk on Indian markets

January to April 2026. With Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur

Does trade policy uncertainty or geopolitical risk move volatility in Indian equity markets? Using 321 months of Nifty 50 returns, I compare GARCH-X, ARDL, SVAR and time-varying models with machine-learning forecasts, and find that neither shock adds much once volatility persistence is accounted for.

Details and findings
  • Assembled a 321-month panel of Nifty 50 returns, news-based uncertainty indices and CEIC macro series, splicing three IIP base years into an HP-filtered output gap and validating outliers against RBI data.
  • Derived and coded a GARCH-X(1,1) likelihood in Python with Hessian-based standard errors and multi-start checks: persistence of 0.94 and no joint gain from TPU and GPR in a likelihood-ratio test (p = 0.48).
  • ARDL bounds tests, a Cholesky SVAR and a Kalman-filter TVP model find a long-run link via US monetary uncertainty (F = 6.75) but no significant TPU effect in any month.
  • LASSO, Ridge, Random Forest and XGBoost lose out of sample to a simple AR(1) (R² 0.79 vs 0.77 and 0.38); Diebold–Mariano tests and SHAP show ML adds no signal beyond persistence.

The cost of rural public-service delivery

September to December 2025. With Prof. Sanjay Choudhari, IIM Indore

How do vehicle capacity and working-time limits shape the cost of reaching remote villages? I replicate a vehicle routing model for 177 delivery locations, trace cost-capacity curves under different time limits, and use Shapley values to show who bears the cost of the network.

Details and findings
  • Replicated a published vehicle routing model in Python for 177 delivery locations; algorithmic optimisation beats judgment-based routing by 15.2%, saving about Rs 1 lakh a year in delivery costs.
  • Cost-capacity curves under 8, 10 and 12-hour limits show that a Rs 20,000 truck retrofit pays back in 6.7 months with no time limit but saves nothing under a strict 10-hour rule.
  • Exact Shapley cost shares for all 177 villages show that the most remote 20% bear 33% of total delivery cost, with a 0.72 rank correlation between each village’s cost and its distance.
  • Results hold under rate, fuel, seasonal and demand shocks and a noisy distance matrix; retrofit payback stays between 4.8 and 9.1 months.

Ujjwala: access versus sustained LPG use in India

October to November 2026

Capstone project with Prof. Vimal Kumar, IIT Kanpur

A policy evaluation of India’s Ujjwala LPG scheme from 2016 to 2026, drawing on 70+ audit, parliamentary and survey sources. 10.58 crore new connections led to just 4.71 refills per household a year; low use tracks free fuelwood, cash-poor households and a connections-only target. September to October 2026

Course project with Prof. Wasim Ahmed, IIT Kanpur

A reproducible pipeline on RBI, MoSPI and Union Budget data from 2004 to 2026. Benchmarks the repo rate against three Taylor rules, finding heavy smoothing and a 2022 tightening that lagged the rule by 3 percentage points; measures pass-through to lending rates (82% to new loans, 56% to existing ones); and shows the link between public capital spending and growth fades within two years.
