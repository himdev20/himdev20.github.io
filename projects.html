Whose words move rates? RBI MPC minutes and Indian yields

With Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur

Tests whether the views individual RBI Monetary Policy Committee members express in the published minutes move Indian interest rates, using an LLM to score the hawkishness of every member statement since 2016.

Key results
  • Scraped all 62 MPC minutes from 2016 to 2026 into 372 member statements with votes, in a tested pipeline that rebuilds every result from raw RBI, FBIL and FRED data.
  • A name-masked LLM matches members’ actual votes in all 372 cases and flags the 40 formal dissenters with an AUC of 0.98.
  • Gürkaynak–Sack–Swanson factors and Jarociński–Karadi sign restrictions show 42% of decisions carry an information effect.
  • A 1-point hawkish gap between members and the Governor lifts the 1-year OIS rate by 2.3 bp on minutes day (p = 0.015).

Ujjwala: access versus sustained LPG use in India

With Prof. Vimal Kumar, IIT Kanpur

A policy evaluation of India’s Ujjwala LPG scheme from 2016 to 2026, built from 70+ audit, parliamentary and survey sources, asking why new connections have not turned into regular use.

Key results
  • 10.58 crore new connections led to just 4.71 refills per household a year.
  • The post-2022 rise in refills fits a price effect but cannot be proven causal, given parallel income and enrolment growth.
  • Low use tracks free fuelwood, cash-poor households and a connections-only target: 25–30% of households took 0–1 refills a year, and state averages ranged from 1.37 (Chhattisgarh) to 5.25 (Haryana).
  • Ranked 5 subsidy reforms, costed 4 scenarios at an extra Rs 2,900–11,900 crore a year, and designed a phased district rollout in which later districts act as the control group.

Monetary and fiscal policy mix in India

With Prof. Wasim Ahmed, IIT Kanpur

A reproducible pipeline on RBI, MoSPI and Union Budget data from 2004 to 2026 that measures how Indian monetary and fiscal policy have worked together.

Key results
  • HP, Hamilton and trend filters give output-gap estimates that differ by over 6 percentage points in 2025.
  • The repo rate shows heavy smoothing, and the 2022 tightening lagged three Taylor rules by 3 percentage points.
  • 82% of repo rate changes reach new loans but only 56% reach existing loans.
  • The link between public capital spending and growth fades within two years; 15 Pytest tests replicate every table in the report.

An LLM assistant for risk-control reviews

American Express, Global Risk and Compliance

A two-stage, document-grounded LLM assistant that automates the second-line review of Risk and Control Self-Assessments, checking whether each process’s controls cover its stated risks.

Key results
  • Processed 750+ controls across 30+ processes in 3+ business units, linking each risk to its controls with risk-based chunking and semantic mapping.
  • Ran 100+ prompt and context-engineering iterations across 5+ model builds, grounding outputs in retrieved documents (RAG).
  • Matched human reviewers on 9 of 12 (75%) and 9 of 11 (82%) judgments, and cut turnaround from 2–3 weeks to about 2 hours.

What fraud does to customer spending

American Express, Global Risk and Compliance

A quasi-experimental test-versus-control study of how third-party fraud changes the spending of 100K+ customers before, during and after a fraud event.

Key results
  • Built 12 metrics and 3 new indices in SQL, including a disengagement score against each customer’s own pre-fraud baseline.
  • Modelled recovery timelines with a normalised slope-based method over a 6-month window.
  • Estimated a ~$37M opportunity cost from fraud-driven disengagement and found a segment losing ~$7.5K every month.

Effect of trade policy and geopolitical risk on Indian markets

With Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur

Asks whether trade policy uncertainty or geopolitical risk moves volatility in Indian equity markets, comparing volatility, time-series and machine-learning models on 321 months of data.

Key results
  • A hand-derived GARCH-X(1,1) shows persistence of 0.94 and no joint gain from TPU and GPR (likelihood-ratio p = 0.48).
  • ARDL, SVAR and Kalman-filter TVP models find a long-run link via US monetary uncertainty (F = 6.75) but no TPU effect in any month.
  • A simple AR(1) beats LASSO, Ridge, Random Forest and XGBoost out of sample (R² 0.79 vs 0.77 and 0.38).

The cost of rural public-service delivery

With Prof. Sanjay Choudhari, IIM Indore

How vehicle capacity and working-time limits shape the cost of reaching 177 remote villages, and which villages bear that cost.

Key results
  • Algorithmic routing beats judgment-based routing by 15.2%, saving about Rs 1 lakh a year.
  • A Rs 20,000 truck retrofit pays back in 6.7 months with no time limit, but saves nothing under a strict 10-hour rule.
  • Exact Shapley cost shares show the most remote 20% of villages bear 33% of total cost.

Applied microeconometric models

With Prof. Arshad Rahman, IIT Kanpur

Discrete-choice, count, censored, selection and panel models, each estimated by maximum likelihood hand-coded with Newton–Raphson in MATLAB.

Key results
  • Probit and logit on 1,240 survey responses: five more years of age cut support for legalisation by 1.6 percentage points.
  • A Poisson model of foot-and-mouth outbreaks across Turkish provinces gives an elasticity to cattle of 0.95 (t = 5.0).
  • Tobit and Heckman models show significant selection into work (ρ = −0.51, t = −4.5).
  • The deterrent effect of executions on US state murder rates (−0.093, p < 0.01) disappears once Texas is dropped (−0.055, p = 0.48).

Impact of atmosphere on agricultural yields in India

With Prof. Anna Thottappilly, IIT Kanpur

Assembling climate, crop, rainfall and satellite data to study how atmospheric change affects Indian crop yields.

Key results
  • Collected 145 years (1880–2024) of NASA monthly temperature-anomaly data to document long-run warming.
  • Worked with a district-year panel of 2,273 observations across 583 districts, 20 states and 23 crops for 2014–2017.
  • Extended it with monthly rainfall for 554 districts and satellite vegetation (SIF) data for 716 districts.
