Whose words move rates? RBI MPC minutes and Indian yields

Aug 2026 to present

Ongoing. Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur. Code

  • Scraped all 62 RBI MPC minutes from 2016 to 2026 and parsed them into 372 member statements with votes, building an end-to-end Python pipeline with unit tests that rebuilds every result from raw RBI, FBIL and FRED data.
  • Scored each statement’s hawkishness with a name-masked LLM that matches the member’s actual vote in all 372 cases and flags the 40 formal dissenters with an AUC of 0.98, benchmarked against dictionary and VADER scores.
  • Separated policy shocks from RBI information effects using Gürkaynak–Sack–Swanson factors and Jarociński–Karadi sign restrictions on OIS and T-bill yields, and found that 42% of decisions carry an information effect.
  • Found that a 1-point hawkish gap between members and the Governor lifts the 1-year OIS rate by 2.3 bp on minutes day (p = 0.015), driven by internal RBI members, with null placebos but a weaker effect outside 2020–21.

Effect of trade policy and geopolitical risk on Indian markets

Jan 2026 to Apr 2026

Prof. Sanjiv Kumar, IIT Kanpur. Report, Code

  • Assembled a 321-month panel of Nifty 50 returns, news-based uncertainty indices and CEIC macro series, ratio-splicing three IIP base years into an HP-filtered output gap and validating every outlier against RBI data.
  • Derived and coded a GARCH-X(1,1) likelihood in Python with Hessian-based standard errors and multi-start checks, finding persistence of 0.94 and no joint gain from TPU and GPR in a likelihood-ratio test (p = 0.48).

Rural public-service delivery cost

Sep 2025 to Dec 2025

Prof. Sanjay Choudhari, IIM Indore. Code

  • Replicated a published vehicle routing model in Python for 177 delivery locations and verified that algorithmic optimisation beats judgment-based routing by 15.2%, saving about Rs 100,000 every year in delivery costs.
  • Estimated cost-capacity curves under 8, 10 and 12-hour time limits and found that a Rs 20,000 truck retrofit fully pays back in 6.7 months when there is no time limit but saves nothing under a strict 10-hour rule.

Ujjwala: access versus sustained LPG usage in India

Oct 2026 to Nov 2026

Capstone project. Prof. Vimal Kumar, IIT Kanpur. Report

  • Co-authored a policy evaluation of India’s Ujjwala LPG scheme for 2016–2026 using 70+ audit, parliamentary and survey sources, showing that 10.58 crore new connections led to just 4.71 refills per household a year.
  • Classified all evidence as administrative, observational or quasi-experimental and showed that the post-2022 rise in refills fits a price effect but cannot be proven causal, given parallel income and enrolment growth.
  • Linked low use to free fuelwood, cash-poor households and a connections-only target, as 25–30% of households took 0–1 refills a year and state averages ranged from 1.37 in Chhattisgarh to 5.25 in Haryana.
  • Ranked 5 subsidy reforms by weighted multi-criteria scoring, costed 4 subsidy scenarios at an extra Rs 2,900–11,900 crore a year and designed a phased district rollout in which later districts act as the control group.
